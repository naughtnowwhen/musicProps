## Model Response

Based on the SPICE simulation results and circuit analysis, here is the diagnosis:

### 1. **Observations**
- Motor voltage dropped **50%** (6.0V → 3.0V)
- Motor current increased **70.5%** (0.44A → 0.75A)
- RPM dropped significantly (1100 → 650)
- **All other measurements match healthy circuit**:  
  - Identical supply voltages (v_supply, v_logic)  
  - Identical loop filter DC and ripple  
  - Normal signal conditioner output (Vpp and waveform)  

### 2. **Analysis**
- **Motor voltage drop + current rise** indicates increased resistance in the motor drive path.  
- **RPM reduction** is a direct consequence of insufficient drive voltage.  
- **Intact control loop** (loop_filter_dc identical) confirms the PLL is generating correct control voltage.  
- **Healthy signal conditioner** rules out feedback issues.  
- Symptoms point to a failure in the **H-bridge power stage**, specifically:  
  - A transistor not fully saturating, creating excessive voltage drop  
  - Increased current flow due to reduced effective drive voltage  

### 3. **Diagnosis**
The faulty component is: **Q1 or Q3 (2N3055 NPN H-bridge transistors)**

### 4. **Explanation**
- **Why Q1/Q3?**  
  The 2N3055 NPN transistors handle high-side switching. If one develops high collector-emitter resistance (due to partial failure or degradation), it would:  
  - Drop voltage across the transistor instead of delivering it to the motor  
  - Reduce effective motor voltage (observed 3.0V vs. 6.0V)  
  - Increase current draw as the motor attempts to maintain speed against higher impedance  
  - Cause RPM drop due to insufficient power  

- **Why not other components?**  
  - **Q2/Q4 (PNP)**: Failure would typically cause total motor cutoff or asymmetric drive, not partial voltage drop.  
  - **Diodes (D1-D4)**: Shorts would cause catastrophic current draw; opens would not reduce voltage.  
  - **Loop filter (R3/C2)**: Matches healthy values → control voltage is correct.  
  - **Op-amp (U2)**: Normal output confirms feedback signal is properly amplified.  
  - **Power supplies**: Identical voltages rule out supply issues.  

- **Symptom consistency**:  
  The specific 50% voltage drop and 70% current increase align with **one high-side transistor** operating in linear mode (not saturated), dissipating excess power instead of delivering it to the motor. This explains all observed deviations while leaving control/feedback systems unaffected.  

**Recommended action**: Replace both Q1 and Q3 (2N3055) due to likelihood of matched aging/stress in H-bridge operation.
